I can’t give you a definitive answer, because the Meridian Works policy isn’t attached here and nothing in this project contains it. Meridian Works isn’t a public policy I can look up, so anything I said about its specific thresholds or exception wording would be a guess.

What I can tell you is what decides the answer. Look for these four things in the policy text:

What the emergency exception actually waives. Some exceptions replace manager approval entirely. Others only waive steps like competitive bids, an approved-vendor requirement, or advance timing. If it only waives the other steps, manager approval is still required and the employee can’t proceed on the exception alone.
Whether there is a fallback approver. Many policies say that if the manager can’t be reached, approval goes to someone else, such as the manager’s manager, a department head, or finance. If yours does, “can’t reach my manager” means going to that person, not skipping approval.
Dollar limits on the exception. Emergency exceptions often apply only up to a certain amount. Check whether $4,000 is within the cap for non-travel purchases.
After-the-fact documentation. Even when advance approval is waived, policies usually require written justification and retroactive approval within a set number of days. The employee would still owe that.

The way the question is framed (“solely under the emergency exception”) suggests the expected answer is probably no. Policies like this typically treat the emergency exception as relaxing how a purchase is made, not as removing the approval requirement. But I’d need to see the actual wording to confirm that.
