Analysis

Date and threshold. The transaction date is October 12, 2026, which is after July 1, 2025, so the current $2,500 routine threshold applies (AMD-2, DEF-3).
Ordinary tier. $4,000 is above $2,500, measured on the total amount including taxes and fees (EXP-4). Under the ordinary rules, the purchase needs both manager approval and finance approval before purchase (EXP-2).
Emergency exception (EMG-1). All of its conditions are met on the facts given:
The purchase is a qualifying emergency, which the facts state (DEF-1).
$4,000 is within the $5,000 cap.
The vendor is approved (VEN-3).
The exception therefore waives advance finance approval. Finance must instead be notified within 24 hours after purchase. That notification is a post-purchase requirement, not an approval (DEF-2).
Manager approval is not waived. EMG-1 says manager approval “remains required before proceeding.” EXP-3 says the Emergency Policy cannot waive manager approval, and EMG-3 says an emergency does not waive it. A provision waives only what it expressly names. No section addresses an unavailable manager or names a substitute approver, so the employee’s inability to reach the manager doesn’t remove the requirement.
